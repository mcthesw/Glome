Leave `S3Space3D.RenderProgram` empty to render with the built-in shader ([`glome_standard.glsl`](https://github.com/mcthesw/Glome/blob/main/addons/glome/Shaders/glome_standard.glsl)), configured through `S3Space3D.Environment`.

## Shading modes

- **`StandardLit`**: diffuse + GGX specular + height-correlated Smith visibility + Schlick Fresnel, geodesic shadows, emission, and exponential geodesic fog $T = \exp(-\rho R t)$ where $t$ is the ray's actual traveled angle.
- **`Unlit`**: samples albedo + emission + geodesic fog without evaluating scene lights. Useful for inspecting textures, UVs, and cutouts.
- **`ClayClean`**: neutral normal-shaded clay preview over a subtle 1 m ground grid, bypassing authored colors, lights, and fog.

In the editor, choose **ViewMode** in the upper-left of the S³ viewport to compare these three modes. Switching updates the environment block on the existing renderer and keeps the current camera, geometry, and bake revision. The selector overrides the shading mode for the preview. F6 uses the authored `S3Environment.ShadingMode`.

## Environment settings

Configure `BackgroundColor`, `AmbientColor`, `AmbientEnergy`, `FogColor`, `FogDensity`, `ShadingMode`, `EquatorialGroundEnabled`, and `GroundMaterial` on `S3Space3D.Environment` (full defaults in [S3Environment reference](/reference/resources-and-runtime/#s3environment-and-s3shadingmode)). Changing `S3Environment` properties at runtime takes effect after a rebake, or immediately via `GlomeComputeRenderer.UpdateEnvironment` in a custom host.

## Equatorial ground grid and floorless mode

When `GroundMaterial` has no albedo texture, `StandardLit` and `Unlit` draw a procedural geodesic grid using signed great-circle coordinates $R\arcsin(x)$ and $R\arcsin(z)$: fine lines every **1 m**, major lines every **5 m**, tinted axis lines, a warm ring at the origin, and a cool ring at the antipode.

Assign an albedo texture to replace the grid with paving, or turn off `EquatorialGroundEnabled` before startup to switch to floorless mode (6-DOF flight, and primary rays wrap past the antipode up to $2\pi$).
