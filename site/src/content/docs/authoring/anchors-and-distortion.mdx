## Why a long wall bunches up near its ends

Godot models meshes in ordinary flat 3D coordinates. To place them on the 3-sphere, each `S3Anchor3D` opens a flat **tangent chart** centered at a point $p$ and projects vertices onto $S^3$ radially from the 4D origin (gnomonic projection).

Near the anchor center, 1 metre in Godot is 1 metre on $S^3$. Farther from the center, each additional metre projects onto a shorter arc along $S^3$: $s(x) = R\arctan(x / R)$.

Suppose you line up nine fence posts spaced **2.00 m** apart along a 16 m wall (`−8 m` to `+8 m`) inside a space with $R = 14\text{ m}$:

- Under **one centered anchor**, the inner span is `1.99 m`, while the outer spans shorten to **`1.60 m`**.
- Split across **two anchors** (each half authored locally from `−4 m` to `+4 m`, placed `3.90 m` from the seam on opposite bearings with the seam post authored once), the outer span recovers to **`1.91 m`**.

## When and how to split

At tangent offset $r = \lVert v \rVert / R$, local radial scale drops by $r^2 / (1 + r^2)$. When $\lVert v \rVert = R / 3$, local radial compression reaches **10%** (`4.67 m` at $R = 14\text{ m}$). Selecting any anchor in the Inspector reports its farthest vertex distance, chart angle, and radial compression.

To split the 16 m wall above at $R = 14\text{ m}$:

1. Divide the mesh into two 8 m halves authored from `−4 m` to `+4 m`, placing the shared center post in one half only.
2. Give each half its own `S3Anchor3D` with `ChartPlacement = ArcMetres`, `ArcMetres = 3.90` ($R\arctan(4/R)$), and opposite bearings (for example `BearingDegrees = -90` and `90`).
3. Keep each half's vertices close to its own anchor origin.

Nesting a child `S3Anchor3D` in `ParentChart` mode with a translated position keeps accumulating offsets in the parent chart rather than opening a fresh center.

## Placement modes

Common authoring modes for `S3Anchor3D.ChartPlacement` (on the equatorial ground, `BearingDegrees = 0°` points toward `+Z` and `90°` toward `+X`):

| Mode | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `ParentChart` (default) | Inherit the nearest enclosing anchor's chart and compose intervening `Node3D` transforms. |
| `SphericalAngles` | Travel `PolarDegrees` along `BearingDegrees`, then lift by `ElevationDegrees`. |
| `ArcMetres` | Travel `ArcMetres` metres along `BearingDegrees`, then lift by `ElevationDegrees`. |

The remaining eight preset modes (`Origin`, `Antipode`, and the six cardinal poles `PositiveX/Y/Z`, `NegativeX/Y/Z`) are listed in [PlacementMode reference](/reference/nodes/#placementmode).

- **`LocalOffset`**: tangent translation in metres (paired with the anchor's `Basis` in absolute modes; mirrors `Position` in `ParentChart`).
- **`RelativeSpin`**: $SO(4)$ displacement authored by the **S³** viewport gizmo, serialized as two unit quaternions (8 doubles: left XYZW, right XYZW).
- **`EditorChartRadius`**: blend radius (m) used by the editor's **From 3D** camera action; does not alter baked geometry.

## Projection math

Given unit chart center $p$, transformed tangent offset $v$ (in metres), and curvature radius $R$, `ChartVertex.Project` maps vertices and inverse-transpose normals $n$ onto $S^3$ as:

$$
q = \frac{p + v/R}{\lVert p + v/R \rVert}, \qquad
N = \frac{n - p(v \cdot n)/R}{\lVert n - p(v \cdot n)/R \rVert}.
$$

This preserves $N \cdot q = 0$ even for off-center or nonuniformly scaled meshes. [Editor workflow](/authoring/editor-workflow/)
